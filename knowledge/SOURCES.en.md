# Sources for the Fungi Mati protocol (v2)

Every operating figure in the protocol is backed by peer-reviewed literature on the genus *Pleurotus*, or was validated in practice: in May 2026, 16 volunteers in the Fungi Mati course grew a bag at home guided by the AI (experiment W4). Citations are kept out of the AI's instructions to keep its answers short; they live here. The full Spanish version, with detailed notes, is in `knowledge/FUENTES.md` in the open repository. ## Parameters and evidence

| Parameter | Value used | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Spawn rate | 10 % of wet substrate weight; 3–5 % works, slower | Bellettini et al. (2019); Bhatti et al. (2007); Larios-Ulloa & Ramírez-Muñoz (2024); Abou Fayssal et al. (2021). Commercial practice 7–10 %. |
| Water : pellet ratio | 1.5 × dry weight (400 g + 600 ml) → ≈ 62–64 % moisture; squeeze test 1–2 drops | Validated in the workshop. Literature range 50–75 %, mold risk above 70 % (Bellettini et al., 2019); 65–75 % optimum on pellets (Wiesnerová et al., 2023). |
| Hydrated lime | 10 g/L (1 %); with pellets all water is absorbed, so lime ends at 1.5 % of dry weight | 1 % best in alkaline soaking (Iossi et al., 2018); up to 2 % best when mixed in, 4–6 % slows growth (Khan et al., 2013; Hernández et al., 2003); *P. ostreatus* tolerates pH 11 while *Trichoderma* barely grows (Romero-Arenas et al., 2012); saturated lime water pH 12.45 (Bates et al., 1956); solubility ≈ 1.5 g/L (Lambert & Clever, 1992). |
| Hot-water pasteurization | Pellets: boil submerged 15–20 min, or pour, cover and insulate ≥ 1 h. Fiber: boil 1 h. Pouring without insulating only hydrates | Insufficient below 55 °C; ~80 °C for < 30 min recommended (González et al., 2022); 63–70 °C for 1.5 h (Grimm et al., 2024); 60–65 °C for 8 h (Abou Fayssal et al., 2021). Lime alone without heat: more contamination (Barrios-Espinoza et al., 2009). |
| Microwave pasteurization | 15 min per kg of soaked substrate, batches ≤ 1 kg | Empirical (author's practice). Microwave kill is thermal but uneven (González et al., 2022); no *Pleurotus*-specific timing study exists. |
| Incubation | 24–28 °C, dark, loose knot or 4–6 pinholes | Spawn run ≈ 24–28 °C (Grimm et al., 2024, after Stamets; Bellettini et al., 2019); mycelium tolerates high CO₂ during colonization (UCCE, 2011); fruiting needs low CO₂ (Lin et al., 2022; Abou Fayssal et al., 2021). |
| Time to colonization / first harvest | 14–21 days / 25–35 days after inoculation | 12–21 days colonization + 3–5 pinning + 4–7 fruiting (Grimm et al., 2024, after Stamets); Bhatti et al. (2007); Larios-Ulloa & Ramírez-Muñoz (2024). |
| Fruiting conditions | Indirect light ~12 h/day, high humidity (mist the space), fresh air | RH 85–95 %; 200–640 lux for 8–12 h; 4–6 air changes per hour (Bellettini et al., 2019; Abou Fayssal et al., 2021; UCCE, 2011). |
| Flushes | 2–3 | Up to 4 reported (Bhatti et al., 2007; Abou Fayssal et al., 2021). |
| Species temperatures | *P. ostreatus* 24–28 / 13–24 °C; var. *columbinus* 21–27 / 12–18 °C; others as in the table | Bellettini et al. (2019, Table 1); Zharare et al. (2010); Mohamed et al. (2016). |
| Substrates | Agave bagasse, hardwood sawdust (pellets), spent coffee grounds, coconut coir; optional N supplement ≤ 20 % | *Agave tequilana* bagasse: 55 % biological efficiency, improves with added nitrogen (Larios-Ulloa & Ramírez-Muñoz, 2024). Sawdust: hardwoods outperform pine (Mihai & Vamanu, 2015; Rugolo et al., 2020; Tisdale et al., 2006). Compressed substrate works (Moreira et al., 2020). Coffee grounds best as 17–33 % additive (Abou Fayssal et al., 2021). Coir (Das et al., 2013). Supplements 3–45 % studied; C/N ≈ 48 best in one study (Ruilova Cueva et al., 2017; Bellettini et al., 2019). |
| Safety and edibility | Cultivated *Pleurotus* from purchased spawn is edible; eat it cooked; ventilate fruiting areas | Sánchez (2010); heat-labile hemolytic proteins (Berne et al., 2009); spore hypersensitivity (Satoh & Yamada, 2022). |
| Household measures | Measuring cup 240 ml, spoons 15/5 ml, level; pellet ≈ 0.65 g/ml, grain spawn ≈ 0.62 g/ml, hydrated lime 0.35–0.64 g/ml | ISO 17225-2 pellet density ≥ 0.60 g/ml; agave–pine pellets ≈ 0.73 (Blancarte-Contreras et al., 2022); lime: PubChem/HSDB; Mississippi Lime (2024). |
